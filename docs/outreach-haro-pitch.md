# HARO / Qwoted journalist-query pitch (ready to send)

How to earn **editorial followed links** from real publications by answering journalist
queries as a **patient-advocate source** (lived experience), not a medical expert.
These are the highest-authority links on the plan — a link from a HuffPost/Parade/
Everyday Health-type article can be worth more than dozens of partner links.

_Created 2026-10-30. See `off-page-link-targets.md`, section A._

---

## Which page to link (match it to the query)
- **Most queries** (living with / navigating / being dismissed with chronic Lyme) →
  the **homepage `alymelife.com`**, with the bio `christina-carter.html` as the
  "who is she" link. A source/bio link is what journalists most reliably keep, and it
  builds the brand/entity authority that also feeds the AI citations.
- **Treatment-specific queries** (hyperthermia, alternative/overseas Lyme treatment) →
  the pillar **`whole-body-hyperthermia-lyme.html`**. Best case: a followed editorial
  link straight to the #1 money page.
- **One clean source link per pitch.** Don't stuff multiple deep links — it reads
  promotional and gets stripped.

## How to win on HARO/Qwoted (the mechanics matter as much as the words)
1. **Speed.** Respond within the hour. Journalists work on deadline and take early,
   complete answers.
2. **Give a ready-to-publish quote.** Don't offer to "hop on a call" and stop there —
   paste a quote they could run as-is.
3. **Stay in her lane.** Patient advocate / lived experience, *not* medical advice.
   It's more credible for these stories and avoids false claims.
4. **No attachments.** Plain text. Photos/credentials only if asked.
5. **Real contact info + fast availability.**
6. Only pitch queries that genuinely fit (chronic illness, Lyme, patient experience,
   medical gaslighting, alternative/overseas treatment, caregiving).

## Swap-in quotes by query angle (keep them lived-experience, never clinical)
- **"Medical gaslighting / being dismissed":** *"For years, every normal test result
  felt like proof I was the problem. No one tells you that 'your labs are fine' and
  'you're fine' are not the same sentence."*
- **"Alternative / overseas treatment":** *"We didn't go overseas for something exotic —
  we went because the standard options had run out and this one hadn't. The decision
  that scared me most was the one that finally helped."* (→ link the hyperthermia pillar)
- **"Cost of chronic illness":** *"The sticker price of treatment stops people before
  they've even figured out whether it's right for them. The money question deserves an
  honest answer, not a sales page."*
- **"Caregiving / supporting a sick spouse":** *"Chronic illness is a two-person job even
  when only one person is sick. My husband carried the logistics I couldn't."*
