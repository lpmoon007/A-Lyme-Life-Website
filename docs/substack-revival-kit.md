## What this is for (set the expectation)
Substack links are **nofollow** — they do **not** raise Authority Score or Google
rankings. That's fine: the job of the Substack is **audience + referral + nurture**.
It's a place to (1) send real humans to the hyperthermia money pages, (2) build an
email list Christina owns, and (3) stay top-of-mind so a reader eventually books a
consult. Treat subscribers as the funnel, not backlinks. (Full rationale lives in
`off-page-link-targets.md`, section B.)

**One rule for every post, forever:** each post links at least once to a
**hyperthermia money page** and once to **book a consult**. That's the "link posts
back to the site" part — it only works if it's a habit.

## Step 1 — fix the profile (10 minutes, do this first)
1. **Publication name:** "A Lyme Life" (match the site).
2. **Short description / tagline:** *Helping chronic-Lyme patients get whole-body
   hyperthermia treatment — one family's hard-won map.*
3. **About page:** 2–3 short paragraphs of Christina's story + a clear line:
   *"The full guides live at **alymelife.com** — start with
   [whole-body hyperthermia](https://alymelife.com/whole-body-hyperthermia-lyme.html),
   or [book a free consult](https://alymelife.com/book.html)."*
4. **Welcome email (Settings → Emails):** turn on the automated welcome and point it
   at the pillar + consult. New subscribers should land on a money page within one click.
5. **Pin** the "We're back" anchor post (below) to the top of the publication.

## Step 2 — cadence
**Every 2 weeks is enough** to look alive and stay in inboxes; weekly if there's
appetite. Consistency beats volume. Batch 3–4 at a time and schedule them so a quiet
month doesn't go dark. Don't chase a backlog — start forward from the anchor post.

## Step 3 — what to publish (rotation)
A simple, repeatable mix so posts are never blank-page hard:
1. **Repurpose a money page** into a personal, story-led version (2 of these are
   written below) → links to that page.
2. **A piece of the Chronicles** — Christina's lived story, serialized → links to
   `chronicles.html` and the pillar.
3. **A practical answer** to a question people actually ask ("How do you afford
   Germany?", "How hot is whole-body hyperthermia, really?") → links to the matching page.
4. **A short "where we are now" note** — keeps subscribers warm, ends with the consult CTA.

The 12 old posts can stay as-is (archive); **don't** delete them — they're the
publication's history. Just start publishing forward.

## Ready-to-paste post 2 — repurpose the Wiegman / hyperthermia page
> **Title:** The treatment nobody told us about: whole-body hyperthermia
> **Subtitle:** What the Wiegman protocol actually is — in plain language

For a long time, "treatment" for us meant another round of antibiotics and another
shrug. What we didn't know was that there's a whole approach built on a simple, almost
old-fashioned idea: **raise the body's core temperature, under medical supervision, to
a point Lyme bacteria don't tolerate well** — and pair it with the supportive care that
makes that safe.

That's whole-body hyperthermia. The **Wiegman protocol** is one of the best-known
versions of it, and it's what first put this on our radar. I've written up what it is,
how a course of it actually runs, and the honest caveats here:
**[The Wiegman protocol for Lyme](https://alymelife.com/blog-wiegman-protocol.html)**.

A few things people always ask me:
- **"How hot are we talking?"** Hot enough to matter, done carefully and monitored the
  whole way — it's a medical procedure, not a sauna. The full guide walks through it.
- **"Is it right for me?"** That depends on your situation, and it's genuinely not for
  everyone. There's a free 5-minute version of the questions I'd ask you:
  **[is hyperthermia right for me?](https://alymelife.com/hyperthermia-self-assessment.html)**
- **"Where do you even get this done?"** Mostly overseas — which is its own story
  (next post).

If you want to skip straight to a conversation,
**[book a free consult](https://alymelife.com/book.html)** and tell me where you are.

*— Christina, [alymelife.com](https://alymelife.com)*

The most common thing I hear after "does hyperthermia work?" is quieter, and harder:

straight, because the money question stops people before they've even looked.

Most whole-body hyperthermia for Lyme is done at specialized clinics in **Germany**.
I've written the practical rundown — why there, what a trip involves, and what it
tends to cost — here:
**[Going to Germany for Lyme treatment](https://alymelife.com/blog-germany.html)**.

What I'll say up front: the sticker number is real, but it's rarely the whole picture.
Families get there in ways that aren't obvious from the outside — and the first step
is almost never writing a check. It's figuring out whether it's even the right path
for you, so you're not fundraising for something that wasn't a fit.

That's exactly what a consult is for. It's free, it's with me, and "we're not sure we
can swing it" is a completely normal place to start:
**[book a free consult](https://alymelife.com/book.html)**.

And if you're deciding this with a partner — bring them. The skeptical questions are
the good ones.
